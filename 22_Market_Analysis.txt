2. MARKET SEGMENTATION & POSITIONING MAP
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Two axes: (a) price/position, (b) ethics/proof.

  Quadrant map (price x ethics-proven *top row = higher*):
    Mass, weak proof     Dove, Bath & Body Works, Life brand
    Mass, strong proof   Dr. Bronner's, some Rocky Mountain lines
    Premium, weak proof  many "clean" boutique lines with vague claims
    Premium, strong proof Lush, Rocky Mountain Soap Co, Pure Anada, B&B

  B&B lane = PREMIUM-ISH PRICE + MAXIMUM PROOF (full labelling, batch codes,
  INCI lists, vegan) at a price BELOW Lush. Our $8 soap vs Lush ~$8.50-14;
  our $10 body butter vs boutique $12-20. Verifiable craft at accessible
  premium = "masstige" — the segment Circana says is growing +22%.

  Collection architecture defends this map: scent story + palette = a
  designed brand, not commodity bars.

3. COMPETITOR ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Direct (artisan vegan, Canada/online):
    Rocky Mountain Soap Co (Canmore AB) - strong brand/pricing, retail stores;
      proof-led. Threat: premium bench; gap: not coastal Ontario/local.
    Pure Anada (Manitoba) - clean/certified, wholesale friendly; boutique
      shelf-share competitor in Ontario.
    Bodylore, Agha Studio, Dip in Nature, Yahoo Soap & Candle Co - direct
      handmade peers; mostly small, mostly regional. Proof quality varies.
    Lush - the proof bar (ingredient stories, bath bomb hero), but premium
      price + less "authentic Ontario craft" story.
  Indirect:
    Mass brands with "natural" SKUs (attract price-first buyers we don't
    chase); drugstore bath bombs ($3-5); Chinese/AliExpress "handmade" soap
    (cheap, zero trust - educated buyers avoid).
  Substitute: nothing replaces the physical hand-made + batch-code proof
  bundle we ship.

  SWOT vs. competitive set:
    Our moat: (1) full CNF-ready formulations, (2) batch traceability + QC
    checklist system, (3) integrated site + costing discipline, (4) Ontario
    local-first loyalty, (5) founder's hands-on maker credibility.
    Their strength to answer: Lush-scale discovery budgets -> we win on
    proof + local + price; boutique shelf reach -> we win on margin
    + storytelling for retailers.

4. PESTLE — CANADA
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Political/Legal: Ontario Business Registry simulation; Federal beauty retail
    rules (Health Canada label/allergen guidance tightening through 2026).
    Grants ecosystem active (SCP, Futurpreneur, PARO, CFDC, digital grants).
  Economic: beauty is counter-cyclical (+6% H1 2026); consumers cautious but
    spending shifts to value-craft; our price is an accessible treat.
  Social: clean/vegan/cruelty-free mainstream; gifting culture strong in
    Eastern Ontario; "Made in Ontario" premium; veteran-free space but crowded
    with small atomic brands -> need scent-design differentiation.
  Technological: social e-commerce + video is the discovery engine for
    craft; CNF e-filing now standard; e-commerce >75% of beauty growth.
  Environmental: minimal/refillable packaging trend; B&B kraft/glass story
    converts Eco buyers; compostable labels where practical.
  Legal note: allergen list grows 24 -> 81 (Aug 2026); cost it.

5. CUSTOMER & CHANNEL ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Revenue mix target (Y3): 45% markets/local, 35% online, 15% wholesale,
  5% custom/corporate. Channels ranked by margin (see file 23):
    1. Markets/pickup            ~84% gross (no shipping)
    2. Custom/wedding            ~70%+ project margin
    3. Online bundles            ~74% net incl. shipping at 4 items
    4. Online singles            ~55-65%  -> de-prioritise, use as bait
    5. Wholesale to boutique     ~65% margin below retail (retailer marks up)

  Churn/retention: bathrooms run out of soap every ~4-6 weeks for regulars;
  subscription/refill + loyalty punch-cards at markets are low-cost retention
  levers.

7. RISK-ADJUSTED VIEW
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  What could make us hit only 60% of plan: slower market calendar (seasonal
  gaps), shipping drag online, producer capacity. Mitigations baked into
  forecasts (file 23): cash-flow reserved for fallow months, bundle-first
  online, batch schedule that front-loads Q4.
  What could 2x the plan: one wholesale pickup (5+ boutiques), one tourism
  contract, holiday bundle breakout. All are upside options, not assumptions.
