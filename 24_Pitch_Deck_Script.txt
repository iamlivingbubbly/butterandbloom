PITCH SCRIPT — Butter & Bloom
13 slides · ~5 minutes + Q&A. Companion to pitch.html.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ELEVATOR PITCH (90 seconds — memorise this)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Butter & Bloom is a small-batch, vegan bath and body brand made in Cornwall,
Ontario. We make soap, bath bombs, steamers, scrubs, butters, and lip balms —
every one fully vegan, every ingredient printed on the label, and every batch
codeable, so a customer can write to us and learn exactly what went into her
jar. The market is growing — handmade soap alone is an 8.3-billion-dollar
global market compounding near five percent. Our economics are the story:
one $450 ingredient batch becomes 802 products and $5,688 in retail revenue
at a 74.4% gross margin. We're asking for $20,000 — $5,000 is already
grant-matched, so the real ask is a $12,000 Futurpreneur-style loan — to fund
equipment, our first batches, and 90 days of working capital. Year one we
project $68,000 in revenue, profitable from the first quarter, doubling each
year after. This isn't a passion project with a business plan — it's a
business plan with a passion."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE-BY-SLIDE SCRIPT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S1  TITLE
    "Butter & Bloom — handmade vegan skincare, Ontario-made. Three numbers to
    start: an 8.3-billion-dollar market, a 74% margin, and a model that turns
    every $450 of ingredients into $5,688 of retail."

S2  THE PROBLEM
    "Clean beauty is marketed, not proven. Mass brands hide ingredients. Craft
    brands either cost Lush prices or are a trust gamble on Etsy. Canada's
    beauty market grew 6% this year, over three-quarters online — proof that
    buyers want more than what the aisle offers, and are online to find it."

S3  THE PRODUCT
    "Six products, one standard: vegan, fully labelled, handcrafted. Soap is
    our hero — cold-process, cured six weeks. Bath bombs and steamers are the
    fun. Body butter and scrub are the ritual. Lip balm is the need-averaging
    entry point. All three scent collections are designed, not accidental."

S4  PROOF
    "Our differentiator is verifiability. Every batch is coded and dated.
    Every label shows full ingredients, bilingual, Health-Canada-disclosing
    fragrance allergens. Written formulas, batch records, and a QC pass before
    anything sells. 'Vegan' here is a receipt, not a slogan."

S5  THE MARKET
    "Handmade soap: 8.3 to 12.5 billion by 2034. Canada's natural and organic
    personal care: 1.75 to 3.03 billion. Canada's bath soap specifically is
    compounding at 9.2%. Etsy handmade soap sales jumped 68%. Skin care is the
    largest clean-beauty slice, and body products are the fastest growing."

S6  POSITIONING
    "Cheap and unproven, premium and unproven, premium and proven — those are
    taken. We own 'mid-price, maximum proof, made in Ontario' — the masstige
    lane, which is growing +22% while prestige lags. $8 soap. Lush-level
    ingredient story."

S7  BUSINESS MODEL
    "Four channels, ranked by margin. Markets and pickup first — 84% gross.
    Online in four-item bundles so shipping doesn't eat us. Wholesale to
    boutiques for shelf reach. Custom and wedding orders at 70%+ project
    margins. Singles online are bait, never the engine."

S8  UNIT ECONOMICS
    "Read this like the pitch's heartbeat: $450 of ingredients yields 802
    pieces across the range, $5,688 retail, $1,457 full cost, 74.4% gross. The
    $4,231 per batch gross is what buys everything else. Margins don't get
    thinner with volume — they get thicker."

S9  FORECAST
    "Twelve batches year one — that's one batch every three weeks, achievable
    solo — $68,256 revenue, $50,767 gross, $24,767 retained after a $12,000
    founder draw. Double batch count in year two, and again in year three.
    Break-even sits at half a batch a month. The base plan runs at twice that."

S10 THE ASK
    "$20,000 total: $7,015 to start — equipment, first batches, regulation,
    setup, launch — and $12,985 working capital for the first 90 days. Funded
    as $2,000 owner cash, a $5,000 Starter Company grant with our $1,250 match,
    and a $12,000 loan. No equity. Loan is interest-only in year one."

S11 THE FOUNDER
    "Why me: I didn't start with a dream, I started with a process. Written
    formulas, QC checklists, batch records, a costing model, and a live site —
    all built before asking for a dollar. I run a careful home studio, and my
    draws are structured so the business self-funds while staying
    ODSP-compliant. Capability plus discipline."

S12 ROADMAP
    "Oct–Nov: register, Futurpreneur application, first CNFs, photos. December:
    first holiday markets and revenue. Jan–Mar: Starter Company application and
    wholesale outreach. June: 12th batch and our break-even review. Twelve
    months to self-sustaining, on daylight milestones."

S13 THE CLOSE
    "The tea is steeping and the soap is curing — the work is real and already
    happening. What we need is the runway to bottle it, sell it, and prove it.
    Butter & Bloom. Thank you."

Q&A PREP
  - "Who is the customer?" Mindful women 30-55, market regulars, gifters,
    bridal/corporate, and SDG tourists. (file 21, profiles P1-P5)
  - "Why will they buy from you and not Lush?" Price below Lush, traceability
    Lush can't match batch-for-batch, truly local Ontario, and collections a
    national brand can't personalise.
  - "What if you can't sell it all?" Sensitivity file 23 shows we stay cash
    positive at -25% volume. Q4 is the season: plan front-loads stock there.
  - "Capacity?" One batch every ~3 weeks solo year one, scaling with a part-
    time helper at roughly double volume in year two.
  - "Regulation?" CNF filings budgeted and instructions ready (file 13);
    bilingual labelling and the expanding allergen list are costed.
